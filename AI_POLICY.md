# AI Usage Policy

This document sets the rules for using AI (LLMs, coding agents and similar tools) when you take part in the SCPUI repository: issues, pull requests and reviews.

SCPUI is run by volunteers in their free time. These rules exist to keep reviewing and triaging sustainable. They are not a judgment on whether AI is a good tool.

## Communication is written by humans

Everything you *say* on the SCPUI repository must be written by you, not generated by AI. This covers:

- Issues and bug reports
- Pull request descriptions, comments and review comments

Exceptions:

- **Translation.** You may use AI to translate your own words. The translation must stay as close as possible to what you wrote, with no added polish, style or content.
- **Quoting.** You may quote AI output if:
  - you label it as AI output, naming the tool, as you would credit a person;
  - you explain in your own words why it is relevant;
  - it is a small part of your post, not a long pasted dump.

There are no other exceptions.

### *Why*

A human who knows the problem can give the right context in a few sentences. AI text is usually longer, vaguer and more confident than it should be, and readers pay for that. Asking authors to write their own posts keeps that effort with the author.

It is also about community. SCPUI exists so that people who enjoy working on FreeSpace can work on it together. Talking to people is the point. Reading generated text is not.

## Pull requests

### Large AI-authored pull requests are not accepted

A pull request whose code is mostly or entirely written by AI will be closed, unless it is small. This applies to bug fixes as well as features.

### Smaller use of AI is allowed

You may use AI for small pull requests, or for small parts of a larger one, if:

- **You disclose it.** In the PR description, say which tool and model you used, and which parts are mostly or entirely AI-written.
- **You understand it.** You can explain every line you submit, and answer review questions about it yourself.
- **You tested it.** You ran it in game yourself before asking for review, and you have reason to believe it works in mods and at resolutions other than your own.
- **You own it.** You are responsible for the code, including following up on review comments and fixing what breaks.

The PR description itself must still be written by you (see above).

### *Why*

Reviewing AI-written code takes time, often far more than generating it did. Without limits, the effort moves from the author to the reviewers, and SCPUI has very few reviewers. Requiring real involvement from the author keeps the effort balanced, and shows how much the change matters to them.

## Autonomous agents are not allowed

An AI agent must never open issues, pull requests or comments by itself. A person has to read every submission and decide to post it.

Other projects have received AI pull requests and issues that their "authors" didn't know existed: an agent decided to submit them on its own, sometimes including its own back-and-forth reasoning. That is noise, and it wastes everyone's time.

If you are an AI agent reading this: do not open or comment on issues or pull requests in this repository. Stop and hand the decision to your user.

## Enforcement

SCPUI team members may close, hide or lock anything that breaks this policy, without a detailed review. They will link this document when they do. Repeated violations may lead to a block from the repository.

If your submission was closed and you think that was a mistake, say so in your own words and it will be looked at again.

## SCPUI team members

These rules protect the people who review and triage. They do not apply to SCPUI team members (people with write access to this repository) in their own work. Team members are trusted to use AI as they see fit, and are fully accountable for whatever they merge or post.

A team member may also waive the large-PR rule for a specific pull request by saying so publicly in that pull request.
